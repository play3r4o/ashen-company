# Originality and Reference Use

The project may learn from references without shipping an imitation.

## Allowed study

- readability at native scale;
- value grouping;
- modular coverage;
- animation economy;
- stable anchors;
- family completeness;
- broad genre/function conventions.

## Prohibited copying

- tracing or paint-over;
- recolor/palette swap;
- splicing pack pixels;
- near-identical silhouette or proportion;
- copied costume, helmet, shield, roof, tree or UI-frame geometry;
- copied pose sequence or animation timing pattern frame-for-frame;
- prompt wording that requests “in the exact style of” a living artist or commercial pack;
- using another game's screenshot as a production layer.

## Three-reference brief

Every substantial recipe uses three roles:

1. **Ashen identity board** — palette, materials, repair language and approved production neighbors.
2. **Functional/context board** — what the asset must do, its projection and how it fits gameplay.
3. **Strong quality comparator** — Tiny Swords sets the finish and readability
   bar only: crisp contours, chunky value groups, material separation,
   modularity, anchors and animation economy. It never supplies Ashen shapes,
   proportions, architecture, palette relationships or pixels.

Never use one commercial pack as both identity and construction reference.

## Identity-delta review

Before production, list at least three deliberate differences in silhouette, construction, proportion, palette relationship, ornament or animation staging. During review:

- compare silhouettes in solid black;
- compare grayscale value masses;
- compare proportions and anchor;
- compare palettes by role, not only exact hex;
- inspect side-by-side at 1×;
- reject any result that a player could reasonably identify as a modified asset-pack element.

## Provenance

Record source name, creator, URL/path, license, date acquired, allowed use and whether pixels were incorporated. Quality comparators must be marked `pixels_used: false`. Unclear license blocks production promotion.
